  Aviation Accident Data Analysis (1983 - 2023)

This project cleans and analyzes commercial and general aviation accident data to help an insurance company figure out which types of airplanes are the safest to buy and underwrite.

 Key Findings & Recommendations

 1. Large Passenger Airplanes (> 20 People Aboard)
Top Picks:BOEING and AIRBUS are the clear choices here.
The Data:Even though these massive brands have a lot of accidents in total because they fly millions of hours worldwide, their actual rate of injuries and plane destruction is incredibly low. 
Distribution:As shown in the plots below, almost all of their incidents sit right at 0.0 on the injury scale. Major injuries are rare outliers, making them a very stable and safe risk for insurance.

![Large Aircraft Risk Profile](large_aircraft_risk.png)

 2. Small Airplanes (≤ 20 People Aboard)
Top Picks:GRUMMAN-SCHWEIZER has the absolute best structural survival rate with a tiny 1.72% destruction rate. AIRBUS 3.08% and DEHAVILLAND (**4.00%**) are also great small fleet choices.
The Data: Smaller planes are much more volatile. Unlike commercial jets, accidents in light planes have a wide vertical spread that goes all the way up to 1.0 (meaning a 100% injury or fatality rate on the flight). 
Takeaway: For smaller fleets, underwriters should prioritize makes with heavy multi-engine usage or highly resilient airframe builds like Grumman.

![Small Aircraft Risk Profile](small_aircraft_risk.png)

Other Factors That Impact Safety
Weather Conditions: Flying in bad weather with low visibility (IMC) more than doubles the rate of passenger injuries and total plane destruction compared to clear skies (VMC). 
Number of Engines: Single-engine aircraft are by far the riskiest assets. Adding just a second engine for redundancy significantly drops both the injury rates and the total loss of the aircraft.
